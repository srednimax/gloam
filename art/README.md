the `VectorDrawable`s.

## The mark

`mark.py` is the single definition, as **two cubic subpaths** — the silhouette and a hole punched
out of it. Both generators import it, so the launcher icon, the notification icon and the feature
graphic cannot drift into being different marks.

What ships today is the moon: a disc with a crescent bitten out of it. The disc is *traced*, not
drawn, so it keeps the concept's slight irregularity rather than becoming a perfect ellipse — and
the crescent is the hole, which is the whole reason the hole has to be real.

- **Recolouring is one place now**: `GROUND_NIGHT`, `GROUND_GLOW` and the glow geometry in
  `mark.py`, read through `mark.ground()`. `make-launcher-icon.py` samples that one function for
  *both* renderings — the `ic_launcher_background.xml` gradient's colour stops and the mipmaps'
  per-pixel raster — so the vector and the bitmaps cannot disagree.
  **This used to be two hand-maintained values** (`PRIMARY` here and a `fillColor` there) with a
  check standing between them. The ground is a gradient now, so that file has real art in it and
  is generated like everything else; the second copy is gone rather than policed.
- **The ground is the app's night with the amber banked low, and that is not a style preference.**
  The first ground was one flat `primary` darkened for contrast and it read as mud — an amber hue
  at a dark *tone* is brown, and a wide flat field of it is brown at its worst. The hue was never
  the problem (the amber is the shade's own warmth), the flat field was. Spend the amber as
  *light* and the mud goes.
- Take the colours from the app's generated scheme (`scripts/gen_scheme.py`) rather than picking
  them for the icon, so the identity and the app agree: `GROUND_NIGHT` is the dark scheme's
  `background` and `GROUND_GLOW` is its `primary` at the tone that reads as a light source.
  `make-feature-graphic.py` **reads** `background` out of `theme/Color.kt`'s dark scheme when it
  renders its text inks, so regenerating the palette and re-running it is the whole of a recolour
  there.
- **The hole must be a real hole in the path**, not a shape painted in the background colour. A
  themed launcher (API 33+) tints the whole monochrome layer one flat colour, and a painted hole
  simply vanishes.

## Where the art has to come from

**Use art you have the right to ship.** This is not a formality — it is the thing most likely to
block a first Play upload, and it is discovered late.

**Where this app's came from**: `concept.png` was generated by an image model from the repo
owner's own prompt on 2026-09-05, and `trace-mark.py` traced it into `mark.py`. `feature-background.png`
— the dusk sky the feature graphic is built on — came from the same place on the same day. Own
prompt, own output, nothing third-party redistributed — and both source files are committed beside
the art they produce, so the claim is checkable rather than remembered.

- An icon traced from an **emoji font** carries that font's licence. Noto Emoji is Apache-2.0 or
  OFL depending on the release, and both impose obligations on the *outlines* — which is what
  tracing copies.
- A **stock icon** is somebody's asset with somebody's terms, whatever the site's marketing says.
- **Art you drew, or art generated from your own prompt, is clean.** Keep a note of which, in this
  file, so the provenance survives you forgetting it.

Rendering *text* with a font is a different question from redistributing its *outlines*: the OFL
explicitly permits documents produced with a font. `make-feature-graphic.py` renders the wordmark
with Noto Sans on that basis and ships no glyph outlines.

## Replacing the mark

```bash
# 1. Draw or generate a concept image, and put it beside this file.
python3 art/trace-mark.py concept.png   # prints cubic path data
#    Paste MARK and EYE into art/mark.py, with your colours.

# 2. Regenerate everything that derives from it.
python3 art/make-launcher-icon.py       # icons, mipmaps, the 512² Play asset
python3 art/make-feature-graphic.py     # the 1024×500 listing graphic
```

Both scripts **measure what they produced** rather than trusting their constants, and the lines they
print are the check: `make-launcher-icon.py` reports the mark's reach against the circular-mask safe
radius, and `make-feature-graphic.py` reports the copy's span against Play's crop and its clearance
from the moon. Read them.

## What Play does to the feature graphic

The asset is 1024×500 (2.048:1) and Play drops it into a **16:9** box with `object-fit: cover` on
the search-results card, so **68px of each side never reaches a viewer** — measured on Binky's live
listing, not guessed. `make-feature-graphic.py` derives `CROP` from the two aspect ratios and lays
out against the cropped frame, leaving a further 40px of margin the viewer actually sees.

This is a mistake worth knowing about because it does not look like one: the graphic is correct in
every preview, in the Console's own uploader, and in the file you open locally. It is only wrong on
the surface most people see it on. The first cut of this file put the wordmark 74px in and the
moon's edge at 973 — 6px of visible left margin, and 17px sliced off the moon.

`make-launcher-icon.py` prints a **safe-radius check** and will tell you when the art is clipped by
a circular launcher mask. It is a real constraint rather than a formality: a launcher may mask the
icon to a 66dp circle, so anything outside a 33dp radius from the centre is cut off. Shrink
`ART_SIZE` until it says `inside`.

Two sizes worth knowing, both already handled:

- The **notification icon** is scaled up relative to the launcher icon. At the launcher's own art
  size the mark would be a speck in a 24dp slot.
- The **flat mipmaps** are cropped to the 72dp a launcher actually shows of the 108dp adaptive
  canvas, so they match what the adaptive icon looks like rather than appearing to shrink.
